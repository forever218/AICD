## 1. AICD and C2PA {#c2pa}

C2PA belongs to the family of technical standards for content provenance and authenticity. Built on X.509 certificates, digital signatures, and hash binding, it provides cryptographic verification of a piece of content's provenance, edit history, and integrity. It addresses rich media such as images, video, audio, and PDF, and the barrier to adoption lies in compliant implementation and a chain of trust. AICD, by contrast, is a voluntary disclosure framework for the degree of AI involvement. It records the A0–A5 level of participation, human review, and tool information through human-readable statements and optional machine-readable fields; it offers no technical verification or certification endorsement; it applies to a broad range of content types, text included; it relies on the publisher's good faith; and it explicitly does not replace mandatory labelling. Put simply, C2PA answers "can the source be trusted, and is it tamper-proof", while AICD answers "is AI involvement transparent".

## 2. Why a "protocol" {#why}

Here, “Protocol” refers to a consensus framework in the soft-law sense—a voluntary normative arrangement. AICD’s mechanism is confined to consensus and disclosure norms; contracting parties, signing procedures, compliance audits, certification mechanisms, and breach liability all lie outside its framework. Its normative force remains at the level of a voluntary initiative, while legal, regulatory, and platform obligations remain independent. It adopts voluntary, open, low-threshold soft-law governance: anyone may adopt or withdraw unilaterally; authorization, registration, and payment are optional; non-adopters continue to follow their own rules; AICD applies only to voluntary adopters, and punitive claims lie outside its mechanism. AICD’s effectiveness derives from good faith, consensus, and reputational mechanisms; enforcement lies outside its governance logic. Its goal is to cultivate a culture of transparency in AI creation; mandatory standards or certification systems belong to other categories of mechanisms. Therefore, it is a protocol—a protocol based on voluntary compliance: it offers choices and normative language, and the scope of obligations is voluntarily accepted by adopters.
